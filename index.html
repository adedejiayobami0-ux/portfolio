Problem

Too many AI tools, but not enough clarity.

Jovita began as an all-in-one AI workspace. The product became harder to explain as more capability was added, so the question shifted from “how many tools can we include?” to “how do people find the right agent for the job?”

Product decisions

Move from broad chat to an agent marketplace.

I worked across positioning, product strategy, feature prioritization, creator tooling, and growth. The current direction organizes agents around outcomes and gives creators a path to package useful workflows.

What shipped

Web, iOS, Android, credits, agents, creator tools.

The product is live across three platforms with real users, credit-based usage, an expanding agent catalog, and an early creator ecosystem.

What I learned

More features do not automatically create a clearer product.

Acquisition, positioning, and retention are different problems. The strongest iteration came from narrowing the story and giving users more concrete jobs to accomplish.

Environment

Launch coordination across 200+ service teams.

The work spans service teams, stakeholder teams, launch assets, readiness dependencies, major events, and executive visibility.

Problem

Coordination cost grows faster than the launch itself.

The challenge is not simply showing status. It is helping different teams know what is blocked, what matters now, and where ownership is unclear.

Approach

Turn fragmented launch signals into a clearer operating workflow.

I use process mapping, stakeholder alignment, tooling, training, and launch mechanisms to make handoffs and readiness easier to manage.

Note

Confidential work, reconstructed responsibly.

03Claude UX ConceptProduct design · interaction thinking
ClaudeUsage

A design exercise in trust

Designing more transparent AI usage limits.

It’s surprise, uncertainty, and the feeling that the product took control away from you. This concept explores how visibility and recovery can change that experience.

UX concept · process below
View Claude design process+
Observation

The pain is uncertainty, not simply the limit.

Usage limits become especially frustrating when users do not know how close they are, what happens next, or when access resets.

Design principle

Make system state visible before it becomes disruptive.

The concept introduces clearer usage status, reset timing, and recovery messaging rather than waiting until the user hits a wall.

Interaction

Calm language over alarm.

The interface avoids punitive messaging and instead explains what is happening, when the state changes, and what options remain.

Goal

Increase trust by returning a sense of control.

The design treats transparency as part of the product experience rather than as an account-settings detail.

I’m most comfortable where the answer isn’t obvious yet.

My background spans product launches, fraud and authentication, consumer experiences, and AI products across AWS, Bank of America, Google, Apple, and Jovita AI.
