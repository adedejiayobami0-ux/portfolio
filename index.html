JOVITA / PRODUCT EVOLUTION
01 / Original hypothesis

One AI workspace for everything.

We believed fragmentation was the problem: people were paying for and switching between too many AI products. So we built breadth.

02 / What we learned

More features didn’t make the value clearer.

The broader the product became, the harder it was to explain why someone should choose Jovita. Capability was growing faster than clarity.

03 / Reframed problem

Users know the outcome. They don’t always know the tool.

That shifted the product from “one chatbot that does everything” toward discovery around jobs to be done.

04 / New direction

An app store for AI agents.

Specialized agents, clear outcomes, and a creator ecosystem that can expand supply beyond what one team can build alone.

02 — How I work

Close to the problem.
Close to the product.

01

Find the tension

What looks like a feature request is often a symptom. I start by understanding the behavior, constraint, or incentive underneath it.

02

Make the decision visible

Flows, prototypes, system maps, rough interfaces — I use artifacts to turn abstract debates into something a team can react to.

03

Ship, learn, change

I care more about what the product teaches us after contact with users than defending the first idea.

03 — About

I’m most comfortable where the answer isn’t obvious yet.

My background spans product launches, fraud and authentication, consumer experiences, and AI products across AWS, Bank of America, Google, Apple, and Jovita AI.

The thread connecting them is ambiguity. I like understanding why a problem exists, deciding what matters, and getting close enough to the product to test the answer.

My design background shapes how I work as a product manager: I care deeply about how a product feels, not only whether it technically works.
